\chapter{The Marginal Scale}
\label{ch:marginal-scale}

\Cref{sec:measured-not-negligible} promised that confidentiality in this
framework would be \emph{measured}, a number on a scale, not a negligible
function in a security parameter. This chapter delivers the number. For a
single cipher value, the leakage is captured by one quantity, the entropy
ratio, and the representation-uniformity parameter $\delta$ of
\Cref{def:uniformity} turns out to lower-bound it through a clean continuity
inequality. The chapter is a summary: the results are the Entropy Ratio
paper's, and the proofs live there; here they are stated, motivated, and
made concrete, with pointers to the standalone work for the full
arguments.\footnote{The marginal-scale theorems, with proofs, are in the
companion \cite{towell2026maxconf}; this chapter paraphrases and cites rather
than reproducing them.}

\section{The Entropy Ratio}
\label{sec:entropy-ratio}

The untrusted machine sees a stream of tokens drawn from the distribution
$Q$ of \Cref{def:uniformity}, supported on the populated set
$\mathrm{im}(\enc)$. The most it could carry, if $Q$ were perfectly flat, is
$H^{*} = \log_2 \lvert \mathrm{im}(\enc)\rvert$ bits per token. How much of
that ceiling the actual traffic reaches is the measure.

\begin{definition}[Entropy ratio]
\label{def:entropy-ratio}
The \emph{entropy ratio} of a cipher map system is
\[
  e \;=\; \frac{H(Q)}{H^{*}},
  \qquad H^{*} = \log_2 \lvert \mathrm{im}(\enc)\rvert,
\]
the observed token entropy as a fraction of the maximum entropy on the
populated support. Equivalently $e = 1 - D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q \,\|\,
U_{\mathrm{im}})/H^{*}$, where $U_{\mathrm{im}}$ is uniform on
$\mathrm{im}(\enc)$. It satisfies $e \in [0, 1]$, with $e = 1$ exactly when
$Q = U_{\mathrm{im}}$, the perfectly uniform, $\delta = 0$ case.
\end{definition}

The normalizer is the log-size of the populated support, $H^{*} =
\log_2\lvert\mathrm{im}(\enc)\rvert$, not the ambient $n$, for the reason
\Cref{def:uniformity} already gave: the traffic lives on the support, and
measuring it against the ambient $\B^n$ would call every sparse construction
maximally leaky and distinguish nothing. The entropy ratio is the
quantitative form of the measured-not-negligible thread: $e$ near $1$ is a
cipher map whose token stream is near-uniform and so leaks almost nothing
about which value each token carries; $e$ small is a stream whose
non-uniformity an adversary can read.

\section{The Fannes Bridge}
\label{sec:fannes-bridge}

The entropy ratio is an information-theoretic quantity; $\delta$ is a
distance. The bridge between them is a continuity inequality, and getting
the right inequality matters.

\begin{proposition}[The Fannes bridge]
\label{prop:fannes}
For a cipher map with representation uniformity $\delta \le \tfrac12$,
\[
  e \;\geq\; 1 - \delta - \frac{h_2(\delta)}{H^{*}},
  \qquad h_2(\delta) = -\delta\log_2\delta - (1-\delta)\log_2(1-\delta),
\]
which is \emph{linear} in $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

The inequality is the Fannes-Audenaert continuity bound, which controls how
much the entropy of a distribution can change under a total-variation
perturbation: two distributions within $\delta$ in total variation differ in
entropy by at most a term linear in $\delta$ plus the binary-entropy
correction $h_2(\delta)$. That is exactly what is needed to turn a bound on
$\delta = \TV(Q, U_{\mathrm{im}})$ into a lower bound on $H(Q)$, hence on
$e$. The tempting alternative, Pinsker's inequality, runs the wrong way: it
lower-bounds Kullback-Leibler divergence given total variation, whereas the
bridge needs an upper bound on the divergence to lower-bound the entropy.
Pinsker would also give a bound quadratic in $\delta$; the correct
Fannes-Audenaert bound is linear, and the linearity is what makes small
$\delta$ buy proportional marginal confidentiality. This is the payoff of
\Cref{def:uniformity}: the parameter that the homophonic machinery drives
down is, through this bridge, a confidentiality guarantee.

\begin{remark}[The marginal single-guess bound, and a warning about names]
\label{rem:le-cam-marginal}
The entropy ratio bounds aggregate leakage; a complementary bound limits a
single guess. A membership-inference attacker who sees one decoded output
and guesses real-versus-filler is held, at a balanced prior, to accuracy at
most $\tfrac12 + \tfrac\delta2$, an advantage of at most $\delta/2$. This is
Le Cam's two-point lemma applied to the cipher-value marginals. The name is
worth flagging, because Le Cam's two-point method reappears at the
compositional scale of \Cref{ch:compositional-scale} in a completely
different role, and the two must not be conflated: here it is a
\emph{marginal} bound and an \emph{upper} bound on a single attacker's
accuracy; there the relevant tool is Assouad's lemma, a \emph{lower} bound
on a minimax estimation rate.
\end{remark}

\section{Three Levers for Delta}
\label{sec:delta-levers}

If $\delta$ is the lever and $e$ the payoff, the question is how to drive
$\delta$ down. There are three constructions, and each pays in a different
currency (\Cref{tab:levers}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Three ways to reduce $\delta$, and what each costs.}
\label{tab:levers}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Lever & Mechanism & Cost \\
\midrule
Noise injection & mix in filler queries, fraction $1-\rho$ & bandwidth; signal diluted by $\rho^2$ \\
Multiplicity & $K(x) \propto D(x)$ encodings per value & space, $\sum_x K(x)$ tokens \\
Granularity & encode tuples jointly (entanglement $p$) & space $O(\lvert Y\rvert^{p})$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Noise injection.}
Pad the query stream with filler drawn uniformly from $\mathrm{im}(\enc)$, so
that a real query (from $D$) appears with probability $\rho$ and filler with
probability $1 - \rho$. The observed token distribution is then the mixture
$\rho D + (1 - \rho) U_{\mathrm{im}}$, and its entropy is the entropy of that
mixture,
\[
  H_{\mathrm{mix}} = H\bigl(\rho D + (1 - \rho) U_{\mathrm{im}}\bigr),
\]
which rises toward $H^{*}$ as $\rho \to 0$. This is the quantity the
untrusted machine actually sees, and it is \emph{not} the source-plus-label
sum $h_2(\rho) + \rho H(D) + (1-\rho) H^{*}$ that one might write down: that
sum is the joint entropy of the token \emph{with} the real-or-filler label,
a label the untrusted machine never observes, so it over-counts the leakage
by the entropy of the hidden label and is only an upper bound. The cost is
that filler consumes bandwidth, and that injected noise dilutes any
signal an adversary could estimate by a factor $\rho^{2}$ in Fisher
information, slowing estimation rather than stopping it.

\paragraph{Multiplicity.}
Assign each value $K(x) \propto D(x)$ encodings, the homophonic prescription
of \Cref{def:uniformity}: frequent values get \emph{more} representations, so
the per-token frequency $D(x)/K(x)$ flattens and $Q$ approaches uniform on
the enlarged support. The direction is the one the historical record
inverted; more demand earns more tokens, not fewer. The cost is space, the
$\sum_x K(x)$ tokens the support must hold.

\paragraph{Granularity.}
Encode several values as one unit, raising the entanglement $p$ of
\Cref{rem:granularity}. This flattens the joint token distribution rather
than the marginals, hiding correlations a component-wise encoding would leak,
at a space cost exponential in $p$. It is the lever that reaches past the
marginal scale, which is exactly why its full payoff is compositional, the
subject of the next chapter.

\section{A Worked Case Study}
\label{sec:case-study}

A small computation fixes the measure, and the paper's full case study fixes
the magnitude.

\begin{example}[Reading the entropy ratio]
\label{ex:entropy-ratio}
Take a populated support of four tokens, so $H^{*} = \log_2 4 = 2$ bits, and
a skewed token distribution $Q = (0.5, 0.25, 0.15, 0.10)$. Its entropy is
$H(Q) \approx 1.74$ bits, so the entropy ratio is $e \approx 1.74/2 \approx
0.87$: the stream carries $87\%$ of the uniform ceiling, and the gap is what
leaks. The total variation to uniform is $\delta = \TV(Q, U_{\mathrm{im}}) =
0.25$, and \Cref{prop:fannes} guarantees $e \ge 1 - 0.25 - h_2(0.25)/2
\approx 0.34$, a conservative floor the actual $0.87$ clears comfortably.
Applying multiplicity, $K \propto D$, splits the heavy tokens and flattens
$Q$ toward uniform, pushing $e$ toward $1$.
\end{example}

At scale the effect is substantial. On a $20$-newsgroups encrypted search
workload, the Entropy Ratio paper drives $e$ from about $0.72$ on the raw
construction to about $0.94$ under homophonic multiplicity combined with
noise injection, at roughly $1.05\times$ the space and $1.5\times$ the
bandwidth.\footnote{The case-study figures, computed with the mixture entropy
$H(\rho D + (1-\rho)U_{\mathrm{im}})$ above and verified numerically, are
Table 4 of \cite{towell2026maxconf}.} The marginal scale, in other words, is
not only measurable but tunable: a designer can buy a known increase in
entropy ratio for a known price in space and bandwidth.

\section{Notes and Provenance}
\label{sec:ch9-notes}

The entropy ratio, the Fannes bridge, and the three reduction constructions
are the Entropy Ratio paper's, summarized here under this part's
state-and-point posture; the proofs are in \cite{towell2026maxconf}. Four of
the book's mathematical landmines are carried in this chapter and are worth
recording as such: the bridge is Fannes-Audenaert and \emph{linear} in
$\delta$, not Pinsker and quadratic; the homophonic prescription is
$K(x)\propto D(x)$, not its inverse; the noise-injection entropy is the
mixture entropy $H(\rho D + (1-\rho)U_{\mathrm{im}})$, not the source-plus-label
sum; and the entropy ratio is normalized by $H^{*} =
\log_2\lvert\mathrm{im}(\enc)\rvert$, image-relative, not by the ambient $n$.
The single-guess bound of \Cref{rem:le-cam-marginal} is Le Cam's, in its
marginal role; the next chapter takes up the compositional scale, where a
different tool governs a rate that none of these marginal levers can move.
