\chapter{Hash-Based Constructions}
\label{ch:hash-constructions}

The abstract parts specified cipher maps without building one. This part
builds them, three ways, and the first is the canonical one: a batch
construction backed by a perfect hash function. It realizes the acceptance
predicate of \Cref{sec:acceptance} directly, hits the space bound of
\Cref{prop:space-duality}, and exists as running code, the
\texttt{trapdoor\_maps} library that backs the experimental claims of the
companion papers. This chapter shows the realization concretely and leans on
Part~II for the abstraction it instantiates.

\section{The Perfect-Hash Backend}
\label{sec:phf-backend}

The seed-searched batch strategy of \Cref{sec:strategies} has a natural
realization in a \emph{minimal perfect hash function}. A minimal perfect
hash for the in-domain set $X$ maps its $\lvert X\rvert$ keys to
$\lvert X\rvert$ distinct slots with no collisions, and an array of those
slots holds, for each key, the codeword of its value. The cipher map $\fhat$
is then three concrete steps: hash the input token to a slot, read the
codeword stored there, return it. Decoding inverts the codeword to a value;
a token that hashes to a slot whose codeword it does not match decodes to
$\bot$.

The seed is the hash's parameter, and the batch search is the search for a
seed under which every in-domain key lands in a slot holding a codeword in
the correct acceptance region $A(f(x))$ of \Cref{def:acceptance}. When such a
seed is found the construction is correct on all of $X$; tolerating a small
error budget $\eta$ lets the search stop sooner, accepting that a few keys
miss their region. The library realizes exactly this: a perfect-hash backend
over the key set, the codeword array, and the seed search, with the value
codec choosing how the slots partition into acceptance regions. It is the
implementation of record for the constructions the book has been describing
in the abstract.

\section{HashSet and the Entropy Map}
\label{sec:instantiations}

One backend yields two instantiations, distinguished by their acceptance
partition (\Cref{tab:instantiations}).

The \emph{HashSet} is the membership construction, $f : X \to \{0, 1\}$. Its
partition has a single accept region: a token decodes to ``member'' when it
hashes to a slot holding the member codeword, and to $\bot$ otherwise. It is
perfectly correct in one direction, $\eta = 0$, every true member is found,
because the perfect hash places it; the only error is the false-positive rate
$\varepsilon$, a non-member token that happens to hash into a member slot.

\begin{example}[The watchlist as a HashSet]
\label{ex:watchlist-hashset}
The watchlist $S = \{\mathrm{alice}, \mathrm{carol}\}$ of
\Cref{ex:watchlist-numbers} is built as a HashSet over the four names: the
perfect hash places alice and carol in member slots, bob and dave in
non-member slots, and the structure is the slot array. A query hashes a name
to its slot and reads membership: alice resolves to ``yes'' with certainty
($\eta = 0$), and a random $12$-bit probe lands in a member slot only with
probability $\varepsilon \approx 10^{-3}$, the noise floor of
\Cref{ex:watchlist-numbers}. The abstract membership map of Part~II is now an
array and a hash.
\end{example}

The \emph{entropy map} is the general construction, $f : X \to Y$. Its
partition has a region $A(y)$ per value, sized $\lvert A(y)\rvert \propto
\Pr[f(X) = y]$ as \Cref{sec:acceptance} prescribed, so that common answers
get large regions. It carries a tunable error budget $\eta > 0$, traded
against build time, and its per-element value cost is the entropy $\mu =
H(Y)$. Where the HashSet answers one bit, the entropy map answers an
arbitrary value, at the Shannon-optimal space.

\section{Correctness and Space, Realized}
\label{sec:realized-cost}

The abstract parameters become measured quantities in the backend. The
correctness budget $\eta$ of \Cref{def:correctness} \emph{is} the Bernoulli
false-negative rate of the underlying approximate-set model: the fraction of
in-domain keys the seed search failed to place correctly, deterministic once
the seed is fixed. The realized space per element meets the duality of
\Cref{prop:space-duality}, $-\log_2\varepsilon + H(Y)$ bits, the hiding term
set by how small a false-positive rate the slots enforce and the value term
by the entropy of the output, and the Bernoulli hash construction is what
achieves that information-theoretic floor rather than merely approaching it.

The one cost the abstraction hid is the search itself. A stricter budget, a
smaller $\eta$, demands a seed that satisfies more constraints, and the
expected number of seeds tried grows accordingly; this is the price the batch
strategy pays up front for its near-optimal space and its tunable accuracy.
The library exposes the trade directly: ask for higher correctness and the
build takes longer, ask for a looser budget and it finishes sooner with a
slightly larger error set. The engineering knob is exactly the
$\eta$-versus-build-time trade the abstract account named.

\section{Notes and Provenance}
\label{sec:ch11-notes}

The perfect-hash batch construction, the HashSet and entropy-map
instantiations, and the space-accuracy realization are the cipher-maps
paper's §6 \cite{towell2026cipher}, made concrete here against the
\texttt{trapdoor\_maps} library that implements them; the chapter realizes
what \Cref{sec:acceptance} and \Cref{prop:space-duality} specified rather
than re-deriving it. The identification of $\eta$ with the Bernoulli
false-negative rate is the hinge to the error theory of the Bernoulli model,
which supplies the false-positive and false-negative accounting the seed
search trades against. The next chapter turns to a construction of a wholly
different character, linear-algebraic rather than searched, where the
frequency-hiding the entropy map buys with multiplicity comes instead from
the structure of a GF(2) system.
